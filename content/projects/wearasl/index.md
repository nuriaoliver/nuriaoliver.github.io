WearASL explored how computer vision and wearable computing could support
two-way communication between people who use American Sign Language (ASL) and
people who communicate through speech. Rather than treating the technology as
separate equipment, the project incorporated its cameras, computer, controls,
displays, speakers, and power supply into clothing and accessories.

## Communication concept

The fashion-show exhibit presented **Van and WearASL** as coordinated wearable
systems. A camera embedded in the hat observed the wearer's hand movements and
sent the images to a computer carried in the backpack. The system was designed
to recognize signed gestures and convert them into synthesized speech played
through speakers in the backpack straps.

For communication in the other direction, a microphone captured spoken input
and the wearable system presented the translated output through a Braille
display. An LCD necklace provided visual feedback so that the wearer could
check the system's interpretation.

## Technical approach

The project poster describes a computer-vision pipeline for segmenting and
tracking the hands, followed by gesture recognition using coupled or single
hidden Markov models. The design distributed the hardware across the outfit:
the camera was integrated into the hat, the computer was carried in the
backpack, and the batteries were stored in the shoes.

## Collaboration and presentation

The project brought together Nuria Oliver, Barbara Rosario, Joshua Weaver, and
Alex Pentland at the MIT Media Lab with Maria Ella Carrera, Ricardo Prado, and
Josefina Batres at Domus Academy in Milan. It was presented in October 1997 as
part of the MIT Media Lab's
[*Beauty and the Bits* Smart Clothes Fashion Show](/projects/smart-clothes-fashion-show/).

The MIT Media Lab's [archived exhibit page](https://www.media.mit.edu/wearables/lizzy/out-in-the-world/beauty/show3.html)
provides the original description of Van and WearASL.
